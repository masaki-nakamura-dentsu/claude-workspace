Don't ask an image model to draw weathering (scratches, mud splatter, dust) or literal illustrated props (rope, canvas tarp, wooden crates) onto a car-concept design, even when the brief is "make it look used/field-worn" or "in-universe military vehicle."

**Why:** Corrected explicitly (2026-08-03, [[toyota_india_aot_hyryder_project]]) on the Attack on Titan × Hyryder "Scout Regiment official vehicle" concept. The user's reasoning: (1) wear/dirt happens naturally once a real car is actually used, so designing it in from the start is pointless and reads as costume/cosplay rather than a real product; (2) illustrated rope/tarp/crate props look cheap and prop-like rather than like real automotive design; (3) a franchise emblem (e.g. Wings of Freedom) that's drawn cleanly in canon should stay clean/crisp in the concept too, not be faked as hand-painted or distressed.

**How to apply:** Express a franchise/world-building theme through things a real car actually has — paint color and finish (matte vs gloss), trim material (leather-tone, brass hardware), and genuine accessory categories (roof racks, skid plates, side steps) — not through illustrated add-ons or surface damage. Keep emblems/logos sharp-edged and precisely rendered exactly as the source material depicts them. This applies broadly to any "themed special edition car" concept work, not just this one project.
